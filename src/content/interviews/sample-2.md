### The Setup
Julian Hayes runs *Neon & Bone*, a fiercely independent design agency known for its tactile, slightly unhinged branding work. When the AI wave hit, his team jumped in with both feet, only to realize their work was losing its edge.

We spoke with Julian about setting boundaries with generative tools, the importance of "ugly" sketches, and why the concept phase is strictly human-only territory at his agency.

**RIDE THE TIGER: You recently implemented a 'No AI' rule for the first 48 hours of any new project. What drove that decision?**

**JULIAN HAYES:** We realized that our initial concepts were starting to look like everyone else's. When you use an image generator or an LLM to brainstorm, you're essentially crowdsourcing from the most average, mathematically probable ideas available on the internet. The algorithm is a mirror reflecting average human thought. If you want an original idea, you have to look somewhere else first.

**RTT: So, back to the sketchpad?**

**JH:** Exactly. We force the team to use sharpies, post-it notes, and bad handwriting. It's intentionally ugly and inefficient. But that friction is where the weird, beautiful, non-obvious ideas live. Once we have a solid, uniquely human concept, *then* we bring in the AI to render it, scale it, or iterate on variations.

**RTT: How do you balance the need for speed with the desire for originality?**

**JH:** It's a constant tension. Clients want things yesterday. But we've found that if we spend more time upfront on the human element, the execution phase with AI is actually much faster and requires fewer revisions. We're not anti-AI; we're pro-intent. You just have to know when to let the machine take the wheel and when to keep it firmly in your own hands.
